Section 1: Failure Points

Failure Point - CEM5530 30-Channel Sample & Hold multiplexer used like VCA's to handle oscillator mixing. The VS uses 2 of these custom CEM chips.
What's The Problem? - They run very hot and are prone to failure, and this heat issue is exacerbated in the cramped quarters of the VS Rack. They are system critical: no CEM5530, no synth.
What To Do If Yours Works - Thank your lucky stars, and then apply heat sinks. Clean the chip, dollop with a little Loctite Super Bonder 498 thermal cycling resistant glue and toss a heat sink on there. There's also talk of Pentium 1 chips being the same size so a fan could work here, need more info. Either way, make plans for when they fail, because they will.
What To Do If Yours Is Broken - You have a few options. One, you could try and find originals but they're few and far between an expensive. Otherwise, you'll need to look into one of the clone options listed down in the Parts Resources. Either way, you either fix it or you own a brick.

Failure Point - Power Supply
What's The Problem? - Prone to failure, also runs hot & buzzes. The heat can cause issues elsewhere in the synth.
What To Do If Yours Works - Kinda depends on what you want to do, you don't HAVE to do anything, but it makes sense to future proof it or at least modify it to throw less heat.
What To Do If Yours Is Broken - You can replace the voltage regulators as a quick fix, but replacing the entire power supply is the best choice. See the resources thread below for options.

Failure Point - Aftertouch (VS Keyboard)
What's The Problem? - The sensors suck, but also receive too much voltage causing them to be even more likely to fail. Also, the chassis doesn't have supports to keep the synth flat so if you play on a keyboard stand, eventually it will bow and fuck up the sensors. This can also cause the circuit board to break too.
What To Do If Yours Works - Always play the synth on a bench, table or other flat surface. Or, if using a keyboard stand, put something hard and flat on the the stand to create a flat surface and place the synth on that.
What To Do If Yours Is Broken - Get it to a tech who can replace the Aftertouch system, at least a few people have had success with new FSR strips. Or ignore it and use a MIDI controller. Better yet, sell it and get a VS Rack instead

Failure Point - LCD Screen
What's The Problem? - It dies
What To Do If Yours Works - Nothing if you don't want to, or upgrade it
What To Do If Yours Is Broken - Replace it with one of the many OLED options available these days, see Parts Resource or just use Google, it's a common fix/upgrade

Failure Point - Data Slider
What's The Problem? - Common break (ever more common to be missing the slider cap)
What To Do If Yours Works - Nothin', upgrade if you want
What To Do If Yours Is Broken - Replace it. Suitable replacements are the ALPS and Bourns listed in the Parts Resources section, not sure if they work with both Rack and Keyboard tho.

Failure Point - Joystick
What's The Problem? - No joy :(
What To Do If Yours Works - Hope it never breaks
What To Do If Yours Is Broken - Good question. The pot tolerance on this is like 20% so it's possible it just needs a rejiggering & calibration. Otherwise....i don't know. They turn up for sale from time to time, but it's usually expensive and they sell quickly. I found mention that Flight of Harmony's Joystick worked as a replacement, but i have no evidence of this.

Failure Point - Cartridge Port (VS Rack)
What's The Problem? - It just doesn't sit well in the rack and tends to get wobby and fail
What To Do If Yours Works - Keep using it.
What To Do If Yours Is Broken? - Good question. I can't find a ton of info on this, Wine Country Sequential is probably your best bet if this dies on you.

Section 2: Parts Resources & Upgrades

General Parts Resources
* Prophet VS parts can be found all over the synth diy web but can also be notoriously tricky and out of stock. I assume you all know how to Google, heh. Aside from eBay, Reverb & VEMIA, the next obviously place to look is Wine Country Sequential. They stock parts and do repairs, OS upgrades & you can pay them to troubleshoot remotely. Occasionally they have 5530 clones, i believe the design of these clones changed a few times over the years.

* There's another replacement version that is a daughterboard utlizing SSM2300s (need more info)
* Jon Leimseider (RIP) of CANTOS was also producing clones, and i've seen one source stating these boards are the SSM2300 boards mentioned above (need more info)
* Wine Country Sequential is always worth checking as well

Curtis Chips
* The VS uses a shitload of CEM's, see the Section 6 below for other synths you can harvest them out of if you're in need. The CEM5530's were already covered under common failures due to their rarity & singular usage. CEM5510's should also be mentioned for the same reason, although the failure rate seems to be much less of an issue, ditto the 3365's.
* The VS used both CEM3379 and CEM3389 filter chips during its production years. 89's are generally regarded as superior so some folks choose to spend the time finding 8 of them and updating if they've got 79's.

Section 3: Aftermarket Accessories & Software

Stereoping
Stereoping make two programmers for the VS
* ProVessorS is a compact MIDI Controller for the VS. 16 knobs & 3 buttons brings programming access for a lot of the VS forward. $550ish new.
* Programmer is a rack unit (3U) that features 45 knobs, 4 encoders & a 2x16 OLED display that brings just about everything on the VS forward. $1100ish new

Vector Surgeon
*Vector Surgeon by Jason Proctor is software that acts as an editor, programming, patch storage & transfer and wave editing & creation.

Cartridges
* Synthtaste makes extended RAM cartridges that can hold 16 (!) banks of programs and waves. $350ish new
* Michael Krause does too
* annnnnd so does Waverex

There are also a ton of various editors, Do a google search, i'm not sure which is the best one to grab but when i do, i'll update

Section 4: OS Differences

Sequential -

v1.0 - Has Diagnostic features and free-running LFO's

v1.1 - This OS removes the Diagnostic features (i think, needs verification)

v1.2 - The most common "final" OS. The has the Arpeggiator & LFO's sync'd to key presses, the biggest arguing point for OS's between users. From the 1987 Update:
1. NON VOLATILE PROGRAM NUMBER: Program Number is now remembered after power off
2. DATA INC/DEC: Data increment/decrement function has been added so that you don't have to make small changes with the slider
3. LFO SYNC: The LFO syncs to key depressions
4. ARPEGGIATOR SYNC: Arpeggios are synced with the playing of the first note! ARP clocking function has been changed so that the clock is reset when the first note is played.
5. MASTER TUNE: Under the master tune screen a field has been added which provides a +\- 12 semitone transposition.
6.LOCAL ON/OFF: Under the MIDI options switch a screen has been added to provide local keyboard enable and disable.

v1.3 - There's rumors that there was a 1.3 released by Sequential that was buggy. Information unknown beyond that.

Not Sequential -

Austin Synth Labs has begun creating new Aftermarket OS's using v1.2 as the base -

v1.3 - Found on GS in 2018, it's 1.2 but with free running LFOs

v1.4 - 1. LFO SYNC: By default the unit has freerunning LFOs. If you change the 8th digit of the patch name to an 'S' (e.g. "Zenny 1S") then both LFOs are synced to the keys. If the 8th digit is an 'X' ("Zenny 1X") only LFO 1 is synced. LFO 2 will freerun.

2. NRPN MSB/LSB IN & OUT: The original software had the byte order flipped LSB->MSB so many controllers (BCR2000, BeatStep Pro, etc) would not function correctly. This has been fixed.

v1.45 - Instead of changing patch name letters for selecting LFO reset, it is now a menu option under "LFO Select" and it is global
- ARP key reset is also now a selectable option under "Option Select"
- ARP Rate MIDI bug has been fixed, so it is now controllable by NRPN.
- Full v1.0 diagnostics are available instead of the slimmed down v1.2 diags.
- The "MIDI Parameter" option now saves in NV memory.
- Fixed my silent wave bug.
- NRPN byte order is flipped. If you contact Gregor at Stereoping he will send you an update to your controller for compatibility.

v1.5 - In planning
